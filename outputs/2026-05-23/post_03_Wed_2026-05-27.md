## Post

**Most engineers treat GPU cost attribution as a FinOps tooling problem. It is not. It is an observability problem. That distinction is costing banks millions in misdirected effort.**

Here is what I keep seeing. Teams at regulated firms spend months building Grafana showback reports and tagging GPU nodes by business unit. Then a CFO asks one simple question: "What does it cost to run a single inference request through our RAG pipeline?" The room goes quiet.

The reason is structural. KServe's InferenceGraph chains one request across three or more pods. First embedding. Then reranking. Then generation. Each pod burns GPU compute. None of them share trace context by default. No standard exists for trace propagation across inference pipelines today. Without that, no FinOps tool can give you accurate per-request cost. The FinOps Foundation's AI and ML Cost Framework draft, published May 2025, does not yet address this gap.

I watched this play out at a Tier 1 investment bank running 20-plus small models on shared A100 infrastructure. The FinOps team built a credible showback report. It was wrong by design. NVIDIA MIG tops out at seven instances per A100. The cost signals never made it out of the serving layer into any telemetry pipeline.

The root problem is upstream instrumentation. Until OTel semantic conventions cover GPU token throughput and model serving spans with cost metadata, the billing picture is fiction. No dashboard fixes that.

Part 4 will cover what actually changes this at the infrastructure layer.

How are your teams handling per-request cost visibility on shared inference clusters today?
