## Post

**Most posts about GPU cost on Kubernetes are solving the wrong problem.**

The conversation keeps circling back to the same framing: move from SageMaker to self-hosted KServe, add Karpenter, watch your GPU bill drop 30-45%. That framing is not wrong. It is just dangerously incomplete.

Here is what vendor blogs and conference recaps are not telling you.

At a Tier 1 investment bank I worked with closely, the CFO's office asked a simple question: how much does each model cost to serve, per business unit, per request? The platform team had KServe running, OpenCost instrumented, and Karpenter bin-packing across GPU families. By any external measure, a mature setup. The answer to the CFO's question was still: we don't know.

The reason had nothing to do with tagging or FinOps tooling gaps. The blocker was structural. KServe's InferenceGraph routes a single request across multiple pods. Embedding, reranking, and generation each run in isolation with no shared trace context by default. That means the cost of one agentic or RAG request is split across three or more pods with no shared identifier to tie them together.

The FinOps Foundation's new AI and ML Cost Framework draft, published May 2025, does not address this. Less than 15% of enterprises have per-request GPU cost visibility in production today. The tooling narrative is quietly skipping over the reason why.

This is not a dashboard problem. It is a platform instrumentation problem with no active standard to fix it.

Part 3 will cover what this means for teams building chargeback into regulated AI platforms right now.

How are your teams handling GPU cost attribution when finance starts asking these questions?
