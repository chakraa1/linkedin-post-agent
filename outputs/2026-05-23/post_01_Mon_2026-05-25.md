## Post

Most engineering leaders assume GPU cost visibility is a FinOps tooling problem. It is not. It is an observability problem, and that distinction is costing firms more than most CFOs realise.

Here is the pattern showing up across Tier 1 banks and trading platforms running shared inference on Kubernetes. The CFO asks how much each model costs per request, per team. The platform team pulls up dashboards. The dashboards show GPU node hours. Nobody can answer the actual question.

The FinOps Foundation published their AI and ML Cost Framework draft last month. Fewer than 15% of enterprises have per-request GPU cost visibility in production today. That number tracks exactly with what practitioners are seeing on the ground.

The structural blocker is upstream, not at the dashboard layer. KServe lets you chain inference steps: embedding, reranking, generation. Each step runs in its own pod. None of those pods share trace context by default. So when a RAG pipeline spans three GPU pods across two teams, cost attribution is not just difficult. It is architecturally impossible with current tooling.

At a major trading platform, this single gap held up a full internal chargeback model for six months. Not because dashboards were missing. Because the right signals were never emitted from the inference layer in the first place.

The FinOps Foundation draft does not address this. The KServe roadmap has an open issue. Most platform teams are still treating it as someone else's problem.

Which team in your organisation actually owns the observability layer for inference pipelines today, platform engineering or the ML team?
