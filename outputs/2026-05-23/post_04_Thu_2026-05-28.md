## Post

**Part 4 of 5: The first fix was not a tool purchase. It was an instrumentation decision.**

In Parts 1-3, I broke down why GPU cost attribution fails in regulated environments. Here is the decision sequence we ran at a Tier 1 investment bank running 20+ inference models across shared Kubernetes clusters.

First, we stopped treating GPU cost as a billing problem. Vendors kept pushing dashboards and tagging. That was the wrong frame. Our inference platform was not sending per-request compute signals into our observability pipeline. No signals meant no spans. No spans meant no FinOps tool could help us. So we started there.

Second, we replaced static GPU node groups with Karpenter NodePools tied to KServe InferenceService SLA tiers. Karpenter bin-packed across A10G and L4 families based on model size and latency class. GPU use moved from the low 30s to above 60 percent in one quarter. Teams using this pattern are seeing 30 to 45 percent cost reduction versus static node groups.

Third, and this is the trade-off most posts skip: we accepted that multi-hop inference pipelines could not be fully attributed yet. There is no W3C Trace Context standard for KServe InferenceGraphs today. We instrumented what we could and flagged the rest as a known gap, not a failure.

Three decisions. Each one unlocked the next. The outcomes were specific and measurable. That is exactly where Part 5 goes.

What instrumentation gaps are your teams running into on inference platforms right now? I'd like to hear what senior engineering leaders are seeing in practice.
