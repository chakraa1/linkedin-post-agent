## Post

**GPU utilisation hit 94%. We still could not tell the CFO which desk owned which cost.**

That is the honest close to five days examining AI inference infrastructure on Kubernetes. And it ties back to the pattern I flagged on Monday: GPU utilisation is a vanity metric, not a cost signal.

At a Tier 1 investment bank, we deployed KServe with Karpenter-driven node provisioning. The headline result was real. GPU spend dropped 38% against static node groups. That number is defensible.

But here is what caught everyone off guard.

The savings landed. The attribution did not. The FinOps Foundation's May 2025 draft found fewer than 15% of enterprises have per-request GPU visibility in production. This team was firmly in the 85%.

The root cause was not tooling. It was observability. KServe pipelines chain multiple model steps together. Embedding, reranking, generation. Each step runs in a separate pod. None of those pods share trace context by default. No propagation standard exists for inference pipelines today. Chargeback across agentic and RAG architectures is not just technically immature. It is structurally blocked.

The decision to expand GPU tenancy across four additional desks was reversed until this is resolved. That was the right call.

This is a platform instrumentation gap, not a dashboard gap. Until that changes, your FinOps tool is reporting fiction. No vendor is being straight about how far away a real fix is.

The savings were real. The accountability layer is still missing.

How are your teams handling GPU chargeback today? Has anyone actually solved per-request attribution in a shared inference environment?
